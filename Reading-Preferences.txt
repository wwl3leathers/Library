READING PREFERENCES — TOUCHSTONE STUDY
October 26, 2026

PURPOSE
A working explanation of what distinguishes Butch's deepest reread favorites from his broader favorites. Use alongside Library.txt when recommending books. This is a preference reference, not a new catalog category or a change to its organization.

RESEARCH FINDINGS — WORKING INTERPRETATIONS
These describe recurring tendencies, not requirements every Touchstone satisfies.

1. Competence changes circumstances.
Learning, ingenuity, persistence, and practical decisions produce consequences beyond a rising power level. A character gains options, independence, responsibility, or the ability to protect others.

2. Becoming trusted and needed.
Relationships give competence consequences beyond personal prestige. Characters acquire responsibilities and a place among recurring people.

3. Power builds something worth keeping.
Homes, communities, teams, institutions, and enduring friendships give success a lasting shape. The appeal often includes what characters can create and sustain after surviving the immediate crisis.

4. Familiarity accumulates alongside power.
Recurring people, ordinary conversations, downtime, and consequences make a world worth returning to after the surprises are known. This is a plausible contributor to rereadability.

5. Humor preserves humanity.
Banter works especially well when grounded in affection, loyalty, personality, or coping with difficulty. The emotional bond behind the joke may matter as much as the joke.

6. Moral worth emerges through conduct.
Official labels such as hero, villain, criminal, or failure need not settle a person's worth. What characters choose to do, and whom they stand beside, can matter more.

7. Extraordinary worlds remain practically understandable.
Accessible storytelling turns fantastic premises into concrete problems and choices. Useful skills, professions, resourcefulness, and intelligible rules help make change feel actionable.

WRITING, AUTHORS, AND AUDIO
The research suggests an interest in character interaction, practical problem-solving, and consequences that persist between adventures. This is a stronger explanation than a simple genre label.

Ten of the 21 Touchstone series/standalone groupings come from four authors: Drew Hayes, Eric Ugland, pirateaba, and Dave Willmarth. This concentration makes their approaches to relationships, humor, competence, and community worth examining when considering recommendations.

Narration can strengthen attachment through distinctive voices, emotional continuity, and comic timing. Author–narrator combinations may matter more than narrator identity alone: some narrators also perform broader favorites that were not elevated to Touchstones.

No common educational background was established. Do not invent a shared college, personal history, or ideology to explain the pattern.

WHAT DISTINGUISHES TOUCHSTONES FROM BROADER FAVORITES?
The likely difference is the combination and emphasis of these qualities. Power progression, community, humor, warmth, or excellent narration alone do not explain the selections.

Useful counterexamples include Noobtown (community and banter), Dungeon Crawler Carl (loyalty, ingenuity, and audio performance), and Beware of Chicken/Heretical Fishing (warmth and everyday life). Butch likes these too. Their placement does not establish why they were not selected as Touchstones, and should not be interpreted as dislike.

RECOMMENDATION GUIDANCE
Use the actual Touchstone selections and comparative research as evidence. Evaluate the combination of practical competence, lasting consequences, recurring relationships, community, humor, prose, and audio performance. Explain recommendations with specific similarities and differences between works, rather than treating any single theme as a mandatory preference.

VIEWPOINT-COMPATIBILITY SCREEN
Apply these checks before presenting a recommendation. They are personal immersion criteria, not judgments about the quality or legitimacy of other stories or readers.

- Prefer an adult human protagonist whose viewpoint Butch can comfortably inhabit.
- An artificial-intelligence protagonist is a poor fit. Portal to Nova Roma was purchased and returned for this reason and for perceived weaknesses in the writing.
- A protagonist confined to a child's body is a poor fit unless meaningful aging occurs early enough for the character to reach at least the teenage years by approximately the midpoint of the first book or story being recommended.
- Isolated references to transgender people or homosexuality are acceptable. Do not recommend a work that focuses repeatedly on either subject, centers a transgender or gay main character, develops a continuing same-sex romantic subplot, or reveals in a later volume that an established main character is transgender or gay.
- Male-female romantic subplots are acceptable.
- Previously tried and rejected or returned works are exclusions even when their themes otherwise appear compatible. Known exclusions currently include Portal to Nova Roma, Ascend Online, and Life Reset.
- When later-volume viewpoint developments could invalidate a recommendation, investigate beyond the first volume. If reliable evidence is unavailable, disclose the uncertainty rather than claiming the series passes.
- Continue to verify present Audible availability and check the current catalog for existing ownership.

EVIDENCE AND LIMITS
The original study compared 21 Touchstone work groupings (101 catalog entries) with 16 Favorited-but-not-Touchstone series (64 entries), using the catalog, selected authorized excerpts, author interviews, and publisher information. It was not a complete rereading, listening comparison, formal stylistic measurement, or statistical test. The explanations remain research hypotheses, not confirmed reasons for individual selections. No single pattern has been shown to reliably distinguish every Touchstone from every broader favorite.
